## 1. Reminiscence therapy (RT)
- **Cochrane review (Woods et al., 2018), "Reminiscence therapy for dementia"**, CD001120.pub3.
  22 RCTs, 1,972 people with dementia. Finding: RT has *some* positive effects on quality of
  life, cognition, communication and possibly mood — **effects are small** and vary by setting
  and format. No evidence of harm to people with dementia.
  Link: https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD001120.pub3/information
- Cochrane's own summary notes RT commonly uses tangible prompts such as **photographs and
  music**, and that research on **digital life-story books and reminiscence apps is still in its
  infancy** — i.e. there is room for exactly this kind of product.
  Link: https://www.cochrane.org/news/featured-review-reminiscence-therapy-dementia
- A narrative analysis of the same 22 studies found RT is **delivered inconsistently**, without a
  shared model. → Product implication: Memory Lane gives a *consistent, structured* session format.
  Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8132012/

## 2. Personalised / individualised music
- **Gerdner's evidence-based protocol "Individualized Music for Elders with Dementia"** —
  includes a preference questionnaire (APMPQ) used to select music from the person's own past.
  → Our profile wizard is a lightweight, Qloo-amplified version of that idea.
  Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3782173
- **Mixed evidence — be honest about it:**
  - A large pragmatic cluster-RCT (54 US nursing homes, 976 residents) found personalised music
    **did not significantly reduce** agitated behaviours or psychotropic drug use.
    Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9256757/
  - A structured-observation analysis from the same programme found **fewer verbally agitated
    behaviours and more observed pleasure** during the intervention.
    Link: https://impactcollaboratory.org/?p=14032
  - A small home-setting pilot found lower agitation while listening to preferred music.

## 3. What this means for positioning (non-negotiable)
- We **do not** claim Memory Lane reduces agitation, slows decline, or treats dementia.
- We **do** claim: it makes personalised reminiscence and music sessions **easier to prepare,
  more personal, more culturally accurate, and better documented** — goals the evidence base and
  care practice already value (quality of life, communication, enjoyment, connection).
- One-line honest framing for README/Devpost:
  > "Reminiscence and personalised music are widely used in dementia care, with evidence of small
  > benefits for quality of life and communication. Their biggest practical barrier is finding
  > material that is truly personal. Memory Lane removes that barrier."

## 4. Cultural gap insight (our unique angle)
- Most existing playlists/tools are Western-centric. Families from India, Latin America, etc. get
  generic content. Qloo's global taste graph + regional signals is the lever.
- Demo includes an Indian persona and US/UK personas to show breadth (judges are US-based).
